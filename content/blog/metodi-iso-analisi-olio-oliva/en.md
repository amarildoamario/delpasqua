## 1. Regulatory Framework: ISO Standards, EU Regulations, and the IOC

The chemical and physical assessment of extra virgin olive oil rests on a two-tiered international regulatory framework designed to safeguard consumer health and guarantee authenticity:

1. **ISO Standards (*International Organization for Standardization*)**: Establish internationally recognized reference analytical methods. They are transposed as harmonized continental (EN ISO) and national standards.
2. **EU Regulation 2568/91 (and subsequent delegated regulations, including EU 2022/2104)**: Sets binding legal limits, physical-chemical characteristics, and testing protocols required for commercial and customs classification across European member states.

In parallel, the **IOC (*International Olive Council*)** in Madrid develops cutting-edge chemical protocols (COI/T.20 series) that frequently pioneer standardizations later adopted by ISO, especially regarding sensory panel evaluation.

Certified testing laboratories (**ISO/IEC 17025**) must operate under rigorous validation parameters, including limits of detection (LOD), limits of quantification (LOQ), repeatability, reproducibility, and uncertainty budgets.

## 2. ISO 660 — Free Acidity and Harvesting Timing

Free acidity is the primary benchmark for assessing olive fruit health and post-harvest care. It quantifies the weight percentage of free fatty acids (FFA), conventionally reported as grams of oleic acid per 100 grams of oil (% m/m).

**Chemical Principle:**
In sound olives, fatty acids remain bound within triglyceride structures. When fruit suffers mechanical damage, olive fruit fly (*Bactrocera oleae*) infestation, or fermentation during delayed milling, endogenous lipase enzymes hydrolyze ester bonds, releasing free fatty acids. Testing relies on an acid-base neutralization titration:

> RCOOH + KOH → RCOOK + H₂O

**Laboratory Procedure:**
Approximately 2.000 g of oil is weighed on an analytical balance and dissolved in 50 mL of a neutralized 1:1 (v/v) mixture of 95% ethanol and diethyl ether. Phenolphthalein indicator is added (colorless to faint pink at pH 8.2), followed by titration against standardized potassium hydroxide solution (KOH 0.1 or 0.5 mol/L) until pink color persists for at least 30 seconds:

> Acidity % = (V × M × 28.2) / m

Where *V* represents titrant volume (mL), *M* is molarity, and *m* is sample mass in grams. For turbid or darkly pigmented samples, ISO 660 and official EU methods mandate potentiometric titration using a combined pH glass electrode.

**Legal Standards and Artisanal Reality:**
European regulations establish an upper limit of **0.8%** for Extra Virgin Olive Oil. However, in fresh artisanal oils cold-pressed within hours of harvest—such as those produced at Frantoio del Pasqua—real acidity consistently falls between **0.15% and 0.25%**, signaling unblemished fruit and pristine extraction.

## 3. ISO 3960 — Peroxide Value and Primary Oxidation

While acidity reflects enzymatic hydrolysis, the Peroxide Value (PV) measures primary oxidative rancidity, quantifying lipid hydroperoxides (ROOH).

**Chemical Principle:**
Exposure to light and dissolved oxygen causes free radical attacks on unsaturated fatty acid bonds. The iodometric method under ISO 3960 exploits the oxidising capacity of hydroperoxides to oxidize iodide ions to free molecular iodine in an acidic medium:

> ROOH + 2 KI + 2 CH₃COOH → ROH + I₂ + 2 CH₃COOK + H₂O

The liberated iodine is immediately titrated back using standardized sodium thiosulfate (Na₂S₂O₃) with a starch indicator solution (observing the sharp clearance of the starch-iodine blue complex):

> I₂ + 2 Na₂S₂O₃ → 2 NaI + Na₂S₄O₆

**Procedure and Critical Factors:**
Approximately 5 g of oil is dissolved in 30 mL of a chloroform/glacial acetic acid mixture (or modern isooctane/acetic acid solvent systems). After adding 0.5 mL of saturated potassium iodide solution, the flask is agitated in complete darkness for 60 seconds. Following addition of distilled water and starch solution, titration is executed promptly.

> De-aerating solvent mixtures with pure nitrogen (N₂) prior to analysis is essential: atmospheric oxygen dissolved in solvents induces auto-oxidation of iodide, resulting in artificial false-positive overestimations.

**Legal Limits and Quality Insights:**
The statutory EU threshold for EVOO is **≤ 20 mEq O₂/kg**. Premium fresh-harvested oils generally measure between **4 and 8 mEq O₂/kg**. Elevated numbers signal initial oxidative breakdown, which eventually degrades into secondary rancid off-flavors.

## 4. ISO 3961 — Iodine Value and Degree of Unsaturation

The Iodine Value (Wijs method) determines the grams of halogen absorbed by 100 grams of fat, providing an aggregate evaluation of total carbon-carbon double bonds across triglyceride acyl chains.

**Principle and Wijs Reagent:**
Wijs reagent (iodine monochloride, ICl, in glacial acetic acid) adds stoichiometrically across unsaturated bonds. Unreacted reagent is subsequently reduced to free iodine by adding potassium iodide and titrated with standardized sodium thiosulfate.

For authentic extra virgin olive oil, the expected iodine value ranges between **75 and 94 g I₂/100 g**. It correlates reliably with fatty acid methyl ester profiles:

> IV = 0.899 × (% C18:1) + 1.810 × (% C18:2) + 2.735 × (% C18:3)

While capillary gas chromatography has largely superseded routine titration, ISO 3961 remains an effective rapid screening tool to detect gross adulteration with highly saturated tropical oils (palm oil, IV < 55) or polyunsaturated seed oils (soybean or sunflower oil, IV > 120).

## 5. ISO 5509 and ISO 12966 — Fatty Acid Methyl Ester Profiles (GC-FID)

The nutritional supremacy of extra virgin olive oil stems from its fatty acid composition. Intact triglycerides cannot be separated directly on gas chromatography columns due to low volatility; ISO 5509 and the updated ISO 12966 standard prescribe their transesterification into volatile Fatty Acid Methyl Esters (FAME).

**Sample Preparation (Rapid Alkaline Transesterification):**
A 0.1 g sample is treated with potassium hydroxide in methanol (KOH 2M). Methoxide ions substitute the glycerol backbone, yielding neutral FAMEs which are rapidly extracted into an upper isooctane layer and neutralized.

**Chromatographic Conditions:**
- Column: High-polarity capillary column (e.g., CP-Sil 88 or DB-Wax, 50 m × 0.25 mm × 0.20 μm) resolving *cis* and *trans* isomers.
- Carrier gas: High-purity Helium or Hydrogen.
- Detector: Flame Ionization Detector (FID) operating at 250°C.

**Statutory Limits for Extra Virgin Olive Oil (EU Regulation):**

| Fatty Acid | Chemical Formula | Permitted Range in EVOO |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Myristic Acid | C14:0 | ≤ 0.03% |
| Palmitic Acid | C16:0 | 7.50 – 20.00% |
| Palmitoleic Acid | C16:1 | 0.30 – 3.50% |
| Heptadecanoic Acid | C17:0 | ≤ 0.40% |
| Stearic Acid | C18:0 | 0.50 – 5.00% |
| Oleic Acid | C18:1 | **55.00 – 83.00%** |
| Linoleic Acid | C18:2 | 2.50 – 21.00% |
| α-Linolenic Acid | C18:3 | **≤ 1.00%** |
| Arachidic Acid | C20:0 | ≤ 0.60% |
| Gadoleic Acid | C20:1 | ≤ 0.50% |
| Behenic Acid | C22:0 | ≤ 0.20% |
| Lignoceric Acid | C24:0 | ≤ 0.20% |

Values deviating from these ranges—especially an α-linolenic percentage exceeding 1.0% or oleic acid falling below 55%—clearly indicate illicit blending with foreign seed oils.

## 6. ISO 27107 — Sterol Composition: The Fingerprint Against Fraud

While fatty acid balances can be artificially mimicked by blending select plant oils, the unsaponifiable sterol fraction represents an unalterable botanical fingerprint of the olive fruit. Standards ISO 27107 and ISO 12228 specify the ultimate method for unmasking complex commercial adulterations.

**Analytical Workflow:**
1. **Saponification:** Refluxing the oil sample with ethanolic KOH for 60 minutes to break ester bonds.
2. **Extraction:** Isolating the unsaponifiable lipid fraction with diethyl ether, washing with water to neutral pH.
3. **Fractionation:** Thin-layer chromatography (TLC) or solid-phase extraction (SPE silica) to isolate the phytosterol band.
4. **Silylation:** Reacting sterols with BSTFA + 1% TMCS at 60°C to create volatile trimethylsilyl (TMS) ether derivatives.
5. **GC-FID Analysis:** Separation on non-polar capillary columns (DB-1 / CP-Sil 5) quantified against an internal standard (α-cholestanol).

**Authenticity Key Markers:**

| Phytosterol | Normal EVOO Range | Fraud Threshold Marker |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Apparent β-Sitosterol | ≥ 93.0% of total | Dominant natural olive phytosterol |
| Campesterol | ≤ 4.0% | If > 4.0% → Canola/rapeseed oil addition |
| Stigmasterol | < Campesterol | If elevated → Soybean oil addition |
| Cholesterol | ≤ 0.5% | Animal fat contamination |
| Δ-7-Stigmastenol | **≤ 0.5%** | If > 0.5% → Sunflower or safflower oil addition |
| Erythrodiol + Uvaol | ≤ 4.5% | If > 4.5% → Pomace oil adulteration |

Even trace additions of commercial seed oils disrupt these precise biological balances, providing undisputed legal grounds for sample rejection.

## 7. ISO 29822 — Wax Content: Distinguishing Mechanical Pressing from Pomace

Waxes are high-molecular-weight esters formed between long-chain aliphatic alcohols and saturated fatty acids. In olives, waxes concentrate within the cuticle layer of the skin to shield against moisture loss.

**Mechanical Pressing vs. Chemical Solvent Extraction:**
During physical cold pressing of intact olives, waxes barely dissolve into the oil. Conversely, when residual olive pomace is treated with petroleum solvents (such as hexane), the solvent strips massive quantities of waxes out of the olive skins.

ISO 29822 separates waxes across hydrated silica gel columns followed by capillary GC quantification:

- **Mechanically Pressed EVOO:** Sum of C40 + C42 + C44 + C46 waxes **≤ 150 mg/kg**.
- **Solvent-Extracted Pomace Oil:** Total wax contents commonly exceed 300 to 1,000 mg/kg.

A wax reading between 150 and 300 mg/kg triggers immediate investigation for illegal blending with deodorized pomace fractions.

## 8. ISO/TS 18363 — 3-MCPD Esters: Detecting Deodorized and Heat-Treated Oils

A notorious commercial fraud involves mild deodorization—vacuum thermal treatment between 180°C and 220°C applied to eliminate sensory defects from defective oils so they can masquerade as extra virgin. Technical specification ISO/TS 18363 provides the analytical tool to verify whether an oil was subjected to thermal rectification.

**The Marker Compounds:**
3-MCPD esters (3-monochloropropanediol esters) and glycidyl esters are process contaminants formed solely under high-temperature refining in the presence of chloride donors.

Using high-resolution GC coupled with triple quadrupole tandem mass spectrometry (GC-MS/MS):
- Unadulterated extra virgin olive oil cold-extracted below 27°C exhibits 3-MCPD levels close to zero.
- Detection of levels above 0.5 mg/kg provides unequivocal proof of unauthorized thermal processing.
